A supervisor moderates streams: the interns on them, the work those interns submit, the logbooks they keep, and the tasks set for them each week. Since 29 September 2026 the ORBIT Agent does that work unattended (Section 01), so nothing here waits on you — but anything you decide first is yours, and the agent leaves it alone. Everything is scoped to your own streams, enforced on the server. Admissions, acceptance letters, accounts and platform settings are admin work and are deliberately absent from this manual.

Section 01

How ORBIT runs itself

Every job that used to need a member of staff is now done by the ORBIT Agent — an automated assistant with its own account (orbit.agent@iih.ng), so every decision it makes is recorded under its name, never yours.

JobWhat the agent doesIf it cannot decide
AdmissionsTwo independent models screen each application; if they agree it is genuine, the agent approves it and places the applicant in the least-busy stream of their first-choice track. Acceptance email (and SIWES letter) follow automatically.A split verdict is admitted after 3 days; an application nobody screened, after 7. Two screeners agreeing on spam or ineligible keeps it out.
MarkingTwo models read each submission — text, files, links — and must agree on a score; a third breaks ties. A unanimous zero (usually work for the wrong task) goes back to the intern to redo.Work it cannot open or agree on is approved at full points 5 days after it was set aside, with feedback saying it was not individually assessed. Anything at all is settled after 14 days.
LogbooksDaily logs are reviewed by two models; one is returned only if both say it describes no real work. Weeks are signed off once they end, have an approved log and nothing is waiting.Split reviews are approved after 5 days; anything after 14.
TasksEach week, for every stream with nothing live, the agent writes fresh tasks and a second model vets each for safety and whether it can be marked. Survivors publish straight away and interns get one message per stream.On Saturdays the prepared task bank tops up any stream the agent missed.
CohortsThree weeks before a cohort ends, the next one is opened as a draft; it becomes active on its start date and the old one closes once it has.—
QuestionsInterns who message the ORBIT Agent get a reply grounded in how ORBIT actually works and in their own record.A reply that fails its check is replaced by a short, safe one.

The timers are the safety net. The agent's reading runs on a machine outside ORBIT three times a day; the rules and timers run inside ORBIT itself. If that machine is off, work is delayed — never stranded, and never left for someone to notice.

What staff can still do

Everything. Staff screens are unchanged, and a person acting first always wins: a submission you review is never touched by the agent, an application you approve is simply approved, and a daily log you sign off is done. The agent only ever picks up what is still waiting.

Telling who decided

  • A submission the agent marked carries feedback ending “Assessed by ORBIT Agent …”; one settled by the timer says it was not individually assessed.
  • An application, daily log or weekly sign-off the agent decided names the ORBIT Agent as its reviewer.
  • Tasks the agent wrote list the ORBIT Agent as their author.
Section 02

Signing in and what you can see

Everyone works from the same address — orbit2.iih.ng — and what you can reach is decided by your role. If a screen described here is missing, your account almost certainly does not carry the role for it; that is a permissions question for a super admin, not a fault.

The four roles

RoleReachesTypical holder
Super adminEverything, plus bulk import and role changesProgramme lead
AdminAdmissions, approvals, letters, placement, streams, tasks, usersProgramme staff
Moderator (supervisor)Their own streams, their interns’ submissions and logbooksTeam leads supervising interns
InternTheir own tasks, submissions, logbook and profileThe students on placement

A moderator only ever sees the streams they moderate. This is enforced on the server, not by hiding buttons — so a supervisor cannot reach another team’s submissions by any route.

Finding your way around

When something is wrong

There is a Report a bug button on every page, bottom right. It takes one sentence and records which screen you were on, which is the part people usually cannot remember later. It works signed out too — useful when the thing that is broken is the sign-in page itself. Anywhere this manual says to raise something, that is the channel.

Do not paste a password or a sign-in link into a bug report. The page address is attached automatically, with anything sensitive stripped out of it.

The green Admin button in the top bar opens the staff area. Inside it, the row of tabs — Overview, Streams, Tasks, Users, Applications, SIWES, Gamification, Settings — is most of the administrative platform, but not all of it. Three things staff use daily sit OUTSIDE it, on the main navigation: Manual (this document, in the app, with a PDF to download), the review dashboard, and the task proposal queue.

Section 03

Streams and tasks

A stream is a team with its own members, feed and work. Tasks are published into a stream, and only its members see them.

Every week the ORBIT Agent writes each stream's tasks. For any stream with no live task, it drafts five against the programme's rules — doable virtually, from public material, safe, and markable from the submission — has a second model vet each one, and publishes the survivors. They are priced by the same complexity rules as a task you write, and every intern in the stream gets one message listing them. You can still add tasks of your own at any time.

Creating a task

  1. Open Admin → Tasks and click Create Task. Admins only — a supervisor has no Admin button; supervisors shape the week by approving or editing proposals in the task queue instead.
  2. Choose the stream — a task with no stream reaches nobody.
  3. Write a title and full instructions.
  4. Check the deadline. It is pre-filled from the cohort’s weekly cadence; change it if this task differs.
  5. Set the points and how work should be submitted — text, link or file.
  6. Click Create Task.

Tasks cannot be created or assigned before the programme starts. If the Create Task button is disabled, check the cohort’s start date — the message on screen names it.

Active and inactive

Deactivating a task withdraws it without destroying submissions already made against it. That is almost always what you want instead of deleting.

Section 04

Reviewing submissions

Submitted work waits on the Review Submissions screen. Supervisors see their own streams; admins see everything.

The ORBIT Agent marks this queue for you, three times a day. What you see here is what it has not reached yet, or has set aside because it could not read the work or its markers disagreed. Reviewing one yourself is always allowed and always wins; the agent never touches a submission a person has decided.

How you get there depends on your role. Supervisors open the profile menu — your avatar, top right — and choose Review Submissions. There is no Admin button on a supervisor account and there is not meant to be; the review screens are reached from the profile menu instead. Admins can use either route.

Making a decision

  1. Filter by status, or search by intern, task or stream.
  2. Click the submission to open it.
  3. Choose Approve, Request Changes or Reject.
  4. For an approval, set the points to award. For anything else, write the feedback — it is required.
  5. Click Submit Review.
DecisionWhat happensPoints
ApproveWork accepted and closedAwarded, up to the task maximum
Request changesReturns to the intern, who can resubmitNone
RejectClosed without acceptanceNone

Feedback is mandatory for anything that is not an approval. An intern who is told only “changes requested” has been given a verdict, not a way forward.

A submission can be resubmitted after Request changes, but not after Approve or Reject — both are final.

Section 05

Logbook and attendance sign-off

SIWES requires a logbook the university will inspect, so this is the part with an external deadline. The Review Dashboard collects everything waiting on a supervisor.

The ORBIT Agent reviews daily logs and signs off weeks, so this dashboard is no longer a to-do list. A log is sent back only when two independent reviewers agree it describes no real work; a week is signed off once it has ended, has at least one approved log, and nothing in it is waiting or was sent back. The sign-off names the ORBIT Agent. You can still review anything that is waiting.

TabWhat is in it
Pending logsDaily logs submitted and waiting to be reviewed
Sign-offsWeekly summaries awaiting a supervisor’s sign-off
My internsThe people you supervise
Missing logsDays that should have a log and do not
AttendanceCheck-ins that need attention — absences and exceptions

Who keeps a logbook

SIWES interns, and nobody else. The logbook exists to be inspected by a university's Industrial Liaison Unit; an internship, NYSC or volunteer participant has no institution expecting one, so ORBIT does not ask them for one, does not remind them about one, and does not count them as missing one on this dashboard.

This is decided by the programme on the person's record. An account created by hand or by import carries no programme, so if a SIWES intern is missing their logbook tabs, that is the field to check first.

Reviewing a log

  1. Open the profile menu (your avatar, top right) and choose Review Logbooks.
  2. On Pending logs, click Review on an entry.
  3. Read the work described, then approve it or send it back with a comment.

Missing logs is the number that matters at the end of the placement. A gap is far easier to resolve in the week it happens than in the month the university asks for the completed logbook.

Verification writes against the intern’s record, not the supervisor’s, so a supervisor signing off work never affects their own logbook.

Section 06

Task proposals: work the agent drafts

The proposal queue matters far less than it used to. The ORBIT Agent now publishes each week's tasks itself, after a second model has vetted them (Section 03), so its tasks no longer wait here. What still arrives as a proposal:

  • The Saturday top-up from the prepared task bank, for any stream the agent did not write for that week.
  • Tasks a person creates through the agent’s connection rather than in the browser.

Proposals publish themselves

A proposal nobody answers publishes itself once the approval window passes (48 hours), with no approver recorded. That keeps a quiet week from becoming a week with no work in it — so an unattended queue is not a problem to solve. You can still step in:

  1. Open Tasks, then Task proposals.
  2. Read the draft — stream, deadline, points and how work should be submitted.
  3. Choose Approve and publish, Edit first if it needs changing, or Decline.
  4. A decline asks for a reason. Write one — it is the only record that survives.

Declining deletes the proposal. There is no declined list and no recycle bin — only your reason, in the audit log. If the task is salvageable, use Edit first instead.

Request another task, above the list, drafts the next prepared task for a stream as a new proposal — useful after a decline.
