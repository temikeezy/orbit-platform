This handbook is for interns on every ORBIT programme — SIWES, internship, NYSC and volunteer. The parts that only apply to some of you, like the logbook, say so. It is written to be looked up rather than read through, so each section stands on its own; start with Section 01 if you are new.

Section 01

Getting started

ORBIT is where your placement happens: your weekly tasks, your submissions and marks, your logbook if you keep one, and your stream's conversations. Everything is at orbit2.iih.ng, on a phone or a computer.

Signing in

  1. Open orbit2.iih.ng.
  2. Sign in with your email and password — or choose Send me a magic link instead and open the link emailed to you. New members have no password yet, so the magic link is how you get in the first time.
  3. Set a password from Settings once you are in, so you do not need a new link every time.

Nobody from ORBIT will ever ask you for your password — not the ORBIT Agent, not staff, not by email.

Your home page

The menu across the top takes you to Community, Streams, Tasks, Logbook, Manual (how the programme works, with this handbook to read or download) and Achievements (the leaderboard and your badges). The bell is your notifications; your picture, top right, opens a menu with your profile, My Messages and settings.

Who runs ORBIT

ORBIT is run by the ORBIT Agent, an automated assistant. It writes each week's tasks, marks your work, reviews your logbook and answers your messages. When something says it was done by the ORBIT Agent, that is what it means — there is no person behind it, and it will tell you so if you ask.

Section 02

Your weekly tasks

Every week your stream gets new tasks, written by the ORBIT Agent for that stream. You get one message listing them when they are published.

  • Pick the ones you want and complete at least two each week. Points add up, so doing more earns more.
  • Deadlines fall at the end of the week — Thursday at 23:59 (WAT) for the current cohort. The task page shows each task’s exact deadline.
  • Each task says how to hand it in: a written answer, a link, or a file.

Taking a task on

  1. Open the task and read the whole brief — it says what to do, what to hand in, and what good looks like.
  2. Press Accept Task.
Section 03

Handing in your work

  1. Open the task you accepted.
  2. Write your answer, paste your link, or choose your file — whichever the task asks for.
  3. Press Submit Task.

Links must open for anyone

Your work is marked by reading it, so a link has to open without signing in. For Google Docs, Sheets, Slides or Drive: Share → General access → Anyone with the link → Viewer.

Folders, private GitHub repositories, Notion, Canva and Figma links usually cannot be read. A PDF, a Word document, or a public link is the safest way to hand work in.

Changing what you handed in

You can change a submission until it is marked — even after the deadline. Open the task and press Edit submission, make your change, and press Update Submission.

Once a task’s deadline has passed you cannot start it, but anything you already submitted can still be edited, and work that was returned to you can still be resubmitted.

Section 04

How your work is marked

Two independent automated markers read each submission against the task’s brief and must agree on a score; if they do not, a third reads it. Marking usually happens within a few days, and can take longer just after a Thursday deadline, when everyone submits at once.

What you seeWhat it means
Approved with a score and feedbackMarked. The points are added to your total.
Changes requestedThe markers agreed the work does not answer this task — usually because it was meant for a different one. Read the feedback, then fix it and submit again.
A notification that your work could not be openedUsually a link that asks for a sign-in. Fix the sharing, or edit the submission. Links are tried again once a day, up to four times.
Approved automatically, “not individually assessed”The markers could not read it or could not agree, so after five days you were given the full points rather than left waiting.

A submission with no work in it — just “done” or “please check” — is never approved automatically. Resubmit it with the work itself.

Marks are final once given. There is no one to appeal to, so the best way to a better mark is the feedback on this one.

Section 05

Points, levels and badges

Every approved submission earns points. Points raise your level, and badges are awarded automatically for milestones — your first completed task, crossing a points threshold, and more.

Section 06

Your logbook

SIWES students keep a logbook, because your institution will inspect it. If your programme does not keep one you will only see Attendance here — checking in when you come to the Hub.

Daily logs

  1. Open Logbook and add a log for the day.
  2. Describe the work you actually did, what you learned and anything that got in the way. Short and plain is fine — it has to be real.
  3. Submit it. The ORBIT Agent reviews it; it is sent back only if it does not describe any real work, and you can edit and resubmit.

Weekly summaries

At the end of each week, write a short summary. It is signed off by the ORBIT Agent once the week is over, at least one of that week’s daily logs is approved, and none is still waiting or sent back. A nearly empty summary is returned for you to fill in.

Your SIWES acceptance letter was attached to your acceptance email as a PDF. Keep it — it is what your institution asks for.

Section 07

Getting help

Message the ORBIT Agent from My Messages (open the menu under your picture, top right) with any question about ORBIT — why a submission has not been marked, how to share a link, when tasks are due. It answers from how ORBIT actually works and from your own record, so it can tell you, for example, exactly why a link could not be opened.

The agent cannot change a mark, extend a deadline, make an exception, or pass your message to a person — those do not exist in ORBIT. It will say so rather than promise something that will not happen.

Notifications

Marks, returned work, new tasks, logbook reviews and replies all arrive as notifications, and most also as an email.

Section 08

Your profile and settings

Settings is where you set or change your password and choose which notifications you receive by email. Your profile — photo, bio, skills — is what other members see.
